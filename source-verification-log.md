# Source Verification Log — Project 2

**Research question:** What post-class review methods help me understand concepts I missed and the connections between them?

## Source 1 — Dunlosky et al. (2013)

- **Source:** John Dunlosky, Katherine A. Rawson, Elizabeth J. Marsh, Mitchell J. Nathan, and Daniel T. Willingham, “Improving Students’ Learning With Effective Learning Techniques: Promising Directions From Cognitive and Educational Psychology,” *Psychological Science in the Public Interest*, 14(1), 2013. https://doi.org/10.1177/1529100612453266
- **Verification:** The publisher page lists the title, authors, journal, and DOI. The abstract and the sections assessing self-explanation, rereading, summarization, practice testing, and distributed practice were checked against the claims in `research-brief.md`.
- **Supported claim:** The review rates practice testing and distributed practice as high utility, self-explanation as moderate utility, and rereading and summarization as low utility overall. “Low utility” is the authors’ assessment across the evidence they reviewed, not a claim that summarization never helps.
- **Limit:** This is a review across many settings. It does not evaluate my exact lecture materials or an AI-made study guide.

## Source 2 — Karpicke and Blunt (2011)

- **Source:** Jeffrey D. Karpicke and Janell R. Blunt, “Retrieval Practice Produces More Learning Than Elaborative Studying With Concept Mapping,” *Science*, 331(6018), 772–775, 2011. https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1199327
- **Verification:** The journal record and author-hosted article identify the authors, title, journal, and DOI. The paper’s central comparison—retrieval practice versus studying with a concept map—was compared with the description in `research-brief.md`. Author-hosted copy: https://learninglab.psych.purdue.edu/downloads/2011/2011_Karpicke_Blunt_Science.pdf
- **Supported claim:** In the learning tasks studied, practicing retrieval led to stronger later learning outcomes than the compared concept-mapping study condition.
- **Limit:** The finding does not mean all concept maps are ineffective or that the same result is guaranteed for my design lectures.

## Claim revised after checking

- **Overstated interpretation considered during this research:** “Summaries and concept maps do not help learning.”
- **Revised version:** Dunlosky et al. gave summarization a low overall utility rating, while noting that it can help in some conditions. Karpicke and Blunt found an advantage for retrieval practice over the concept-mapping study condition they tested. Neither source establishes that a visual study guide is useless. A visual layout can organize the material, followed by questions answered from memory to check understanding.
